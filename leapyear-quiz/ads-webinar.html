The bigger question

Senior year keeps asking the wrong question.

For most families, senior year collapses into one question:

Where are they going next?

That question matters. But by the time it gets answered — applications, deposits, decisions — your student is usually past the point of asking the deeper one:

Who are they becoming?

A student can be smart, accepted, talented, and loved by God, and still not be ready for the freedom, pressure, distraction, and quiet identity-shaping that waits for them after high school. The pattern isn't that students are unintelligent. It's that they're being asked to commit to a direction before they know who they are.

For Christian families, the stakes are higher. You've spent years caring about formation, not just achievement — praying, teaching, investing, trying to help your student become the kind of person who can live faithfully in the world. Then, suddenly, the system asks them to pick a school, a major, a path, and a future — and everyone acts like that's normal.

The framework

The Launch Readiness Framework

Most families ask, "What should my senior do next?" The Launch Readiness Framework asks a different question entirely:

What needs to be true of them before they launch?

In the webinar, we walk through the three areas every 18-year-old has to develop before they step into the freedom of what comes next.

01 · Career Clarity

What am I built for — and what should I actually try next?

Not a locked-in major. Not a forced career plan. Not pressure to have life figured out at 18. Career clarity is the difference between picking a path because the system asks you to and choosing one because you've actually tested it.

It means your student understands how they're wired. It means they've seen real work up close — not in a brochure, in a room. It means they've talked to real practitioners and tested their own assumptions against reality. And it means there are adults around them who can interpret what they're noticing back to them.

The students who land well after high school usually have one thing in common: by the time they pick a direction, they've already ruled out two or three others — with evidence, not anxiety.

02 · Leadership Capacity

Can they take responsibility for the life God has given them?

A student can be academically excellent and still not be ready to lead their own life. Leadership capacity is about ownership — the daily, unglamorous kind that no GPA ever measures.

Can they manage their own time without being told? Can they walk into a room with adults and hold their own? Can they make a plan, follow through, reflect, and try again — when nobody is checking? Can they move toward hard conversations instead of around them?

This isn't about becoming impressive. It's about becoming the kind of person who can be trusted with freedom. The world is full of capable students who don't yet know how. After high school, no one tells them — they just hand them the keys.

03 · Spiritual Formation

Who will they become when they get there?

This is the deepest layer. Your senior isn't just choosing a college, a major, or a job path. They're becoming someone — and after high school, that becoming gets faster.

New freedoms. New friends. New temptations. New loneliness. New pressure to perform. New ideas about what their life is for. Most students step into all of it without a rule of life, without spiritual practices that can hold weight, without adults around them whose faith is integrated with their work.

For Christian parents, the question can't only be, "Where will they go?" It has to be who they will be when they get there — and what it will take to help them build conviction, practice faithfulness, and stay rooted in God as the pace picks up.

About LeapYear

Forming the next generation of leaders

LeapYear is a 9-month leadership accelerator for students after high school. It exists because formation doesn't come from a syllabus — and the year after high school, when most of the real becoming happens, is the year families have the least visibility into.

The program is built on five counter-cultural commitments:

  • Formation > Information. Wisdom, not just knowledge.
  • Exemplars over Experts. Lives worth imitating, not lectures to take notes on.
  • Learn by doing. Real experiments, real stakes, real feedback.
  • Community is the curriculum. A cohort that knows you and calls you up.
  • High expectations + high support. A bar worthy of who they could become — and someone walking next to them the whole way.

Students spend the year discovering how they're built, testing career paths through real work, building a rule of life and spiritual practices that can hold weight, and stepping into adulthood with more clarity, courage, and direction than a classroom alone can give them.

The founding cohort included students who deferred offers from Stanford, Purdue, and Texas A&M.

Common questions

A few things parents ask

Is this only for families already interested in LeapYear?

No. This is for Christian parents who want to think clearly about their student's next step. We'll talk about LeapYear, but the main goal is to give you a framework you can use either way.

My student is a junior — is this for us?

Yes. Junior year is actually the best time to start thinking about launch readiness, because the framework is most useful when you still have time to act on it. The year before senior year is when families have the most space to be intentional about it.

Is this anti-college?

No. College can be a wonderful next step. The question is whether your student is ready to use that opportunity well.

Some students should go straight to college. Some would benefit from a structured year first. The point is to make that decision with wisdom, not pressure.

What if my student already knows where they're going?

This is still worth your time. A college decision doesn't automatically answer the deeper questions of maturity, ownership, faith, and direction. If your student is headed to college, this framework will help you think about what they still need to build before they go.

Can't we just give them this at home?

You've been giving them this their whole life — and you should keep doing it. But there's a developmental reality at 18: most students need to hear the same values from an adult who isn't you before they fully internalize them. That's not a failure of your parenting. It's how this stage works. LeapYear (and this webinar) is designed to come alongside what you've already built, not replace it.

Is this a sales pitch?

We'll explain LeapYear and answer questions about the program. But the webinar is designed to be useful even if LeapYear isn't the right fit for your family.

Is this for parents or students?

Primarily parents. Students are welcome to join, especially if they're part of the decision. But the conversation is designed for parents carrying the weight of helping their student launch well.
